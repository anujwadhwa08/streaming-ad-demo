Agentic Streaming TV
Ad Platform

A working system where one AI agent makes the final ad decision — supported by rule-based filtering, a live auction engine, and behavioral session intelligence.

The Problems This Explores
01
Ad load stays fixed even when viewer engagement changes completely
Someone three episodes deep into a binge is not the same viewer as someone in their first 10 minutes. Static ad schedules don't know the difference — they serve the same break count regardless of session depth, completion likelihood, or churn risk. A system should adapt.
02
When an ad underperforms, nobody knows exactly why
Was it the wrong audience segment? The wrong time of day? Poor content fit? Frequency fatigue? Traditional ad pipelines return a winner — not a reason. Without a reasoning trace per decision, optimization is guesswork. Auditability needs to be first-class, not an afterthought.
03
Context is rich at decision time — and almost none of it gets used
The system already knows: what's playing, who's watching, what time it is, how long the session has been running, and what ads have already shown. The auction picks a price winner. But a price winner isn't always the right ad for this viewer, at this moment, in this content. Reasoning over the full context is the gap.
04
LLMs reason well — but what does "AI in a real-time ad pipeline" actually look like built?
Latency limits, guardrails, state machine validation, structured outputs, audit logging — these constraints shape every product decision when you put AI in the critical path. This system is an attempt to answer that question with a working implementation, not a concept.
What This Explores
The Question

Programmatic CTV ad decisioning is a mature, complex space — real-time bidding, audience targeting, deal prioritization, frequency capping. This demo asks a different question: what does the decisioning layer look like when an LLM is the final decision-maker? Not replacing the auction or the targeting stack — sitting above it, reasoning across the outputs and explaining its call.

What Was Built

A working pipeline where one Claude Sonnet agent receives the auction winner, content metadata, viewer signals, and time-of-day context — and produces a final decision with a written rationale stored per impression. The focus: can an LLM act as a reasoning layer in a real-time ad pipeline, and can every decision it makes be fully auditable?

How the Pipeline Works
1
Ad Opportunity Detected
A trigger fires — a scheduled break in a show, a viewer pausing, or a broadcast cue signal (SCTE-35 for live TV). The system collects context: content genre, time of day, viewer segment, session history.
Trigger Layer
2
Inventory Filter — Rule-Based
A JavaScript module filters all live campaigns down to eligible candidates: correct audience segment, content type allowed, budget remaining. No AI here — fast, deterministic rule evaluation.
Rule-Based Filter
3
Compliance Check — Rule-Based
Another module enforces policy rules: hourly and daily frequency caps, same-campaign restrictions, competitive separation (no two rival brands in the same break). Anything in violation is removed before auction.
Rule-Based Check
4
Second-Price Auction
Eligible campaigns bid in a Vickrey (second-price) auction — the same mechanism used by Google Ads. The highest bidder wins but pays the second-highest price plus one cent. Transparent and fair.
Vickrey Auction · Math
5
AI Decision Agent — The Only AI Step
Claude Sonnet receives the auction winner, context scores, viewer profile, and content metadata. It reasons about the full picture and makes the final ad selection — writing out its reasoning in plain language. Every decision is saved to an audit log with the full reasoning trace.
Claude Sonnet · Decision Agent
6
Ad Serves. Impression Logged.
The winning ad plays in the viewer's stream. A tracking module logs the impression, fires VAST beacons, and updates campaign spend in the database. No AI — deterministic event logging.
Event Logging · No AI
AI Concepts Used
🤖
Agentic AI
"AI that takes actions, not just answers questions"
The Decision Agent doesn't just return a score — it reasons through the full context, selects a winner, and writes an explanation. It operates autonomously within a defined pipeline, taking an action (ad selection) that has real downstream effects.
📋
Structured Outputs + Audit Logging
"AI that explains every decision it makes"
The agent returns a structured decision — campaign selected, reasoning, confidence. Every decision is stored in an audit log with the full reasoning trace. This is what makes AI trustworthy in a production ad system.
🛡️
State Machine Guardrails
"AI proposes. System validates. System executes."
The agent never acts directly. Every proposal passes through a state machine that validates the transition before execution. This keeps the system auditable and prevents the agent from taking invalid or unsafe actions.
🧠
Context-Aware Decisioning
"AI that reads the room before deciding"
Before the agent makes its call, each campaign is scored for contextual relevance: content genre match, time-of-day fit, viewer segment alignment. These scores influence both the auction and the agent's final reasoning.
⚡
Pre-Computation for VOD
"Run AI offline so delivery is instant"
For Video On Demand, context scores are pre-computed offline and cached. When the viewer reaches an ad break, the decision is already ready — no live AI latency in the playback path. Trade-off: speed over recency.
📈
Behavioral Session Intelligence
"System adapts to how you watch, not just what you watch"
The binge frequency model reduces ad breaks as a session deepens (3→2→2→1 across episodes). Idle for 30+ minutes or a new login resets the count. Ad load becomes a function of engagement — not a fixed schedule.
Three Ad Formats — All Decisioned by the Same Agent
▶️
Break Ads
Mid-roll spots at scheduled breaks in a show. Non-skippable. Full pipeline runs for each break. Frequency suppressed intelligently as the viewer binge-watches.
⏸️
Pause Ads
Triggered 1.5s after the viewer pauses. Two sub-formats: brand overlay (services, financial) or shoppable product card (PLA) linking to a product detail page.
📺
SSAI / DAI
Server-Side Ad Insertion — ads are stitched into the video stream before it reaches the viewer. Unskippable, seamless, indistinguishable from content.

Key Product Decisions Made
Why only one AI agent?
Inventory and compliance are deterministic rule checks — AI adds no value and introduces unpredictability. The Decision Agent is where context and reasoning actually matter: weighing auction results, content fit, and viewer signals simultaneously.
Why non-skippable ads?
Non-skippable ads are the norm in premium streaming — the trade-off for the viewer is fewer, shorter breaks. The trade-off for the platform is committing to that contract. Binge frequency reduction is what makes it sustainable across a long session.
Why second-price (Vickrey) auction?
Industry standard for programmatic advertising. Advertisers bid their true value without strategic underbidding. Transparent, fair, and maximizes long-term platform trust.
Why does the session break threshold matter?
30 minutes idle signals the viewer has left and returned — a new attention window justifies a full ad load reset. Anything shorter would punish viewers for brief interruptions. Anything longer loses revenue unnecessarily.
